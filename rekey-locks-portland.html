When rekeying makes sense

Rekeying is useful when the lock body and hardware are still in serviceable condition but the existing keys should stop working. The cylinder is adjusted to accept a new key combination, giving the property a new working key without replacing every compatible lock.

It is not always the right answer. Damaged, weak, badly worn, incompatible, or outdated hardware may need repair or replacement. A locksmith should inspect the locks before recommending the cheapest or fastest option.

Good reasons to rekey

  • You purchased or moved into a property
  • A tenant, employee, contractor, or roommate kept a key
  • Keys were lost, stolen, or copied without control
  • You want several compatible locks to use one key
  • The hardware works well but access needs to change

Does rekeying replace the entire lock?

No. Rekeying changes the cylinder so old keys stop working. The existing hardware remains if it is compatible and in good condition.

Can different locks use the same key?

Often yes, when the lock brands and keyways are compatible. Photos or an inspection are needed before confirming.

Should I rekey after buying a home?

Rekeying is a practical way to prevent unknown old keys from operating suitable existing locks after a move.
