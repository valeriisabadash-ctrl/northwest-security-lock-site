Smart lock installation starts with the door—not the app.

A smart lock can be excellent hardware or a constant headache. Reliable operation depends on door alignment, backset, bore size, handing, existing hardware, battery access, connectivity and how the property actually needs to control entry.

Before buying a device, send photos of both sides of the door and the edge. If the existing locks only need a new working key, Portland lock rekeying may be simpler. For broader home lock and hardware work, see our residential locksmith service. Multi-door commercial electronic entry may be better suited to access control in Portland. If the door itself is damaged or fails to latch, commercial properties should start with commercial door service.

Questions to answer before installing a smart lock

  • Is the door residential, rental, office, storefront or common-area entry?
  • Do users need codes, phone access, physical keys or all three?
  • Does the property need temporary or trackable access?
  • Is Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, wired power or standalone operation preferred?
  • Does the current door close and latch correctly?

Can you install a smart lock I already bought?

Often yes, if the device is compatible with the door and existing hardware. Send the model number and photos before scheduling.

Do smart locks still have physical keys?

Some do and some do not. The best choice depends on backup-access needs, property type and security preferences.

Can a smart lock fix a door that does not latch correctly?

No. Door alignment and latch problems should be corrected first or the smart lock may fail, drain batteries or jam.

Do businesses need smart locks or a full access-control system?

That depends on the number of doors, users, schedules and access levels. A single keypad may be enough for a simple opening; larger properties may need a planned access-control system.
