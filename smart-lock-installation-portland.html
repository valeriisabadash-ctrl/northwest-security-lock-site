The lock must fit the door, not just the app

A smart lock can be excellent hardware or a constant headache. The result depends on door alignment, backset, bore size, handing, existing hardware, connectivity, battery access, and the way users enter the property.

Before buying a device, send photos of both sides of the door and the edge. For commercial doors, include the frame, latch, closer, exit hardware, and power availability. Compatibility should be confirmed before installation.

Questions to answer first

  • Is the door residential, rental, office, storefront, or common-area entry?
  • Do users need codes, phone access, physical keys, or all three?
  • Does the property need temporary or trackable access?
  • Is Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, wired power, or standalone operation preferred?
  • Does the current door close and latch correctly?

Can you install a smart lock I already bought?

Often yes, if the device is compatible with the door and existing hardware. Send the model number and photos before scheduling.

Do smart locks still have physical keys?

Some do and some do not. The best choice depends on backup-access needs, property type, and security preferences.

Can a smart lock fix a door that does not latch correctly?

No. Door alignment and latch problems should be corrected first or the smart lock may fail, drain batteries, or jam.
